What began as a series of unexplained memories has become something far more unsettling.

Emma can no longer dismiss the inconsistencies surrounding her life as coincidence. Conversations she remembers before they happen, familiar experiences she cannot explain, and fragments of a past that seemingly never existed are forcing her to question the very nature of her reality.

But she is no longer alone.

As Ethan begins experiencing anomalies of his own, their growing connection becomes intertwined with a mystery neither of them fully understands. Together, they search for patterns within their memories, hoping to uncover an explanation for experiences that defy conventional reasoning.

Their investigation draws them deeper into Professor Evelyn Bennett’s research and forgotten histories. What once appeared to be unrelated discoveries begin to converge, suggesting connections that challenge everything Emma believes about memory, identity, and time.

Every answer leads to another question. Every discovery makes coincidence less convincing. And every familiar moment carries the unsettling possibility that their lives have intersected before.

As the boundaries between memory and reality become increasingly uncertain, Emma must confront a possibility more frightening than the memories themselves:

What if the life she remembers isn’t the only life she’s lived?

With Ethan beside her, Emma struggles to determine what is real, what can be trusted, and whether the connection they share belongs to the present—or to something far greater than either of them can comprehend.

But discovering the truth may come at a cost neither is prepared to accept.

EMMA: Convergence is the second installment in J.P. Rogers’s psychological thriller series. Blending psychological suspense, mystery, speculative fiction, and an increasingly complex love story, it explores the fragile relationship between memory and identity, the nature of human connection, and the possibility that some encounters are never truly accidental.

When separate paths begin to converge, the most dangerous discovery may be where they ultimately lead.
