The EMMA Series · Book I

EMMA Awakening

J.P. Rogers · Psychological Thriller

Some memories aren’t yours.

Nineteen-year-old Emma has always believed that memory is what makes us who we are. But what happens when the memories you carry belong to a life you don’t remember living?

A forensic psychology student at St. John’s University in Queens, New York, Emma is accustomed to searching for logical explanations for human behavior. Her world revolves around her studies, her family, and the familiar routines of university life.

Until those routines begin to feel a little too familiar.

Conversations she somehow knows before they happen. Places she remembers visiting for the first time. Fleeting sensations of recognition that leave her questioning whether her mind is playing tricks on her or revealing something she was never meant to discover.

When Emma meets Ethan, an unexpected connection develops between them. There is something inexplicably familiar about him, a sense of recognition that extends beyond attraction or coincidence. As their relationship deepens, so does Emma’s uncertainty about the memories she cannot explain.

Determined to understand what is happening to her, Emma begins investigating the psychological and neurological explanations behind her experiences. But her search leads her toward Professor Evelyn Bennett, whose research raises unsettling questions about the nature of memory, consciousness, and human experience.

As Emma discovers inconsistencies within her own past and begins questioning the things her family has never told her, the distinction between what she remembers and what she knows becomes increasingly fragile.

The deeper she investigates, the more she realizes that the greatest mystery may not be what her mind is remembering, but what it has forgotten.

And some truths may be more dangerous to remember than to lose.

EMMA: Awakening is the first installment in J.P. Rogers’s psychological thriller series, blending psychological suspense, speculative mystery, romance, and the unsettling phenomenon of déjà vécu into an exploration of memory, identity, and the possibility that our lives may not be as unfamiliar as we believe.

What if déjà vu isn’t a mistake in your memory?

What if it’s the first indication that you’ve been here before?
